      The first day begins at the golden crescent of Onetahuti Beach, where the water shifts from amber-clear shallows to vivid turquoise — a colour that defines the entire park. The track weaves through coastal native forest, descending repeatedly to secluded beaches fringed with driftwood and pohutukawa before climbing back above the sea. From the higher sections, granite islets rise sharply from the deep blue water below, capped with bush and surrounded by breaking swells. The day ends at the popular Anchorage Bay, one of the largest beaches on the track, where boats and kayaks share the calm anchorage.

      The second day heads south through some of the track's most photogenic scenery. Hidden bays appear through gaps in the tree ferns — small, perfectly sheltered coves of turquoise water almost entirely enclosed by forest — before the track drops to beaches scattered with large granite boulders smoothed by centuries of tide. The forest interior offers a quieter contrast, with streams crossing mossy root networks beneath a closed canopy. The track finishes at Marahau with views across Tasman Bay toward the forested islands that dot the coast, bringing the full sweep of the park into one final frame.
